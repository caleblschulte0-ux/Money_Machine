# Offer

## 1. Pay Per Lead — $85

**Deliverable:** Exclusive delivery of a qualified enquiry matching the buyer's
trade and service area, with full enquiry details and a 24-hour dispute window.

**Outcome claimed:** Each lead is exclusive to one buyer, screened for
duplicates and spam, and disputable within 24 hours.

**Explicitly not claimed:**
- That the lead will answer the phone or book a job
- Any close rate or revenue
- Any lead volume in any period

## 2. Exclusive Monthly Territory — $2,500/month

**Deliverable:** All qualified leads for one trade in an agreed set of postal
codes for a calendar month, with a monthly report showing delivered, accepted
and disputed counts.

**Outcome claimed:** Every qualified lead generated in the agreed territory goes
to this buyer and no other during the term.

**Explicitly not claimed:**
- Any minimum number of leads
- Job value or close rate
- Exclusivity beyond our own channels — the buyer's other suppliers are their
  business

## The volume non-claim is the important one

Promising volume is the standard mistake. Volume depends on ad performance,
seasonality and weather — a hail storm produces a month of leads, and a quiet
February does not. A buyer who was promised 40 leads and receives 22 disputes
the invoice regardless of quality.

Every buyer agreement must state that no volume is guaranteed. It is in the
manifest's legal notes for that reason.

## What "qualified" means, precisely

Defined by the scoring model, not by judgement:

- Property **owner** (renters are disqualified — they cannot authorise work)
- Phone number supplied
- Postal code in the service area
- A described roofing need (leak, storm, hail, missing shingles, replacement)
- Not a solicitation

Threshold: 55 of 100. Below that the lead is not delivered, and every score
carries its reasons — which is what makes a dispute resolvable in one message.

## Disputes

`assessDispute()` auto-credits objective, verifiable reasons: duplicate, wrong
service, outside area, invalid contact, spam. Subjective reasons go to a human.

A buyer whose dispute rate exceeds 40% is routed to a conversation rather than
another credit — that is a relationship problem, not a credit decision.
