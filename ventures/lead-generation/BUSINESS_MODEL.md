# Business model

## Revenue

| Model | Price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pay per lead | $85 | Exclusive, with a 24-hour dispute window |
| Exclusive monthly territory | $2,500/month | All qualified leads for one trade in agreed postal codes |

Territory is the better business: predictable revenue, a buyer invested in
making it work, and no per-lead haggling. Per-lead is the entry point that
proves quality first.

## Cost structure

| Cost | Nature |
| --- | --- |
| Paid advertising | Variable — the dominant cost |
| Landing page and tracking infrastructure | Fixed, small |
| Call tracking | Variable |
| Lead qualification | Variable, automated |
| Dispute handling | Variable, partly human |

Everything turns on one number: **cost per qualified lead** against what buyers
will pay.

## The economic bet

That paid acquisition can produce a qualified roofing lead for meaningfully
under $85 in one metro. If cost per qualified lead approaches the price, there
is no business — and no amount of volume fixes it, because volume scales the
loss.

This is knowable with roughly $2,000 of ad spend, which is why the venture is
cheap to test and expensive to assume.

## Why exclusivity matters commercially

Selling the same lead to four buyers quadruples revenue per lead and destroys
the business. The contractor who calls fourth learns they are fourth, and buys
elsewhere. `routeLead()` refuses to route a lead twice, at the service level,
with a test.

## Where it feeds the portfolio

- Lead-gen sites bring automation agency customers — the contractors buying
  leads have exactly the operational problems the agency solves
- Call and form data becomes market intelligence about a trade
- The AI call centre venture, if built, improves the conversion of these leads
  and can be sold to the same buyers
