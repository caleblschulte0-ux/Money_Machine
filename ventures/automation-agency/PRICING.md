# Pricing

| Package | Price | Interval |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automation Audit | $2,500 | one-time |
| Starter Automation | $3,500/mo + $1,500 setup | monthly (planned) |
| Department Automation | $12,000/mo + $2,500 setup | monthly |
| Full Operations Automation | quoted | monthly (planned) |
| Custom Enterprise | quoted | quoted (planned) |

Only the audit and department packages exist as plans today.

## Reasoning

**The audit is priced to qualify, not to profit.** $2,500 is small enough to
approve without a committee and large enough that only a serious buyer pays it.
A free audit attracts people who want a free opinion; the conversion difference
is the entire point.

**$12,000/month is anchored on a fraction of the labour cost it addresses.** A
department spending 200 hours a month on manual handoffs is spending roughly
$8,000–$12,000 in loaded labour on that work. Charging a comparable amount to
address a portion of it is defensible arithmetic — and honest, because we do
not claim to eliminate that cost, only to reduce the hours.

**The setup fee covers integration work that is real and non-recurring.**
Waiving it teaches clients that implementation is free.

**Usage above the allowance is passed through at cost plus margin**, tracked
per customer in the cost ledger. Absorbing inference cost is how a services
business discovers its best customer is its least profitable one.

## What is not done

- **No discounting to close.** A discount granted under pressure sets the price
  for the renewal. `discount.grant` is a high-risk action requiring approval,
  which makes the decision visible rather than reflexive.
- **No annual prepayment discount** until churn is known. Prepaid annual
  contracts hide churn for twelve months.
- **No percentage-of-savings pricing.** It requires agreeing on a
  counterfactual, and every dispute becomes an argument about the baseline.

## Review triggers

Revisit pricing when any of these is true:

- Close rate above 50% — priced too low
- Close rate below 15% with qualified prospects — priced too high, or the offer
  is unclear
- Gross margin below 50% for two months — a kill criterion
- Delivery hours per audit not falling by the tenth audit — the model, not the
  price, is the problem
