# Customer

## Who buys

A 20–200 person company, $3M–$50M revenue, in a trade or field-service industry
— HVAC, roofing, commercial doors, excavation, equipment rental, specialty
contracting. Regional, often single-location.

The buyer is the **owner, president, COO, controller or operations manager** —
someone who signs and who personally feels the pain. Below that level there is
interest but no budget.

## The situation

They run on 3–6 systems that do not talk to each other: a field service or job
management tool, QuickBooks or Sage, Outlook or Gmail, a shared drive, and
several spreadsheets that one person maintains. Between each pair sits a human
retyping data.

They are not anti-technology. They have usually bought software that did not
get adopted, which makes them sceptical of anything that requires their crew to
change behaviour.

## What they say

- "Sarah spends two days a month just entering invoices from job tickets."
- "I don't know if a job made money until the month closes."
- "We lose quotes because nobody follows up."
- "Every report I get, someone built by hand in Excel."

They describe **specific people doing specific repetitive things**. That is the
signal the scoring model looks for: a prospect who can state hours per month has
a baseline, and a baseline is what makes a savings claim defensible.

## Who does not buy

| Not a fit | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Under 20 employees | Not enough process volume to justify the price |
| Over 500 employees | Procurement cycle exceeds our capacity |
| Pre-revenue or pre-process | Nothing to automate yet |
| "Replace my staff" | Outside what this venture sells; the scoring model disqualifies it |
| Researchers, students, competitors | Not buyers |

## How they are reached

1. **Referral from a completed project.** Best conversion, slowest to start.
2. **Direct outbound** to named operations roles at companies in the target
   trades, referencing something specific and verified. The SDR agent drafts;
   a human sends.
3. **Content** on the specific handoffs that waste time in these trades.
4. **Trade associations and local business groups.**

Cold volume is not the strategy. A hundred well-researched approaches beat a
thousand generic ones, and the compliance layer caps frequency per contact
regardless.

## What earns trust

Naming what will *not* change. A prospect who has been sold "AI transformation"
before responds to someone who says which three specific handoffs will be
automated, which will still need a human, and what the number was before.
